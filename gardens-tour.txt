Before you go in (11:30)
You're about to walk through a park that used to be a lake. Princes Street Gardens lie in the valley between the Old Town and the New Town, right under the castle rock. Here's the short version of why.
The valley held the Nor Loch, an artificial loch created in the early fifteenth century by building a dam. It was part of the town's defences. It was also, according to the record, habitually polluted by sewage running down from the Old Town. When the New Town was built, starting in the seventeen sixties, the loch was drained, and the gardens were created in the eighteen twenties.

Princes Street Gardens (11:30)
There are two gardens, split by the Mound, the hill where the National Gallery stands. The east gardens are small, about eight and a half acres. The west gardens, where you are, cover twenty nine acres and run out to the churches of St John's and St Cuthbert's.

Ross Fountain (11:40)
This is the Ross Fountain, and it has an unusual story. It's made of cast iron, and it was produced at the foundry of Antoine Durenne in Sommevoire, in France. It was shown at the Great Exhibition of eighteen sixty two.
A local gunmaker, Daniel Ross, bought it there for two thousand pounds, and then gave it to the city of Edinburgh. It was shipped to Leith in eighteen sixty nine in a hundred and twenty two pieces, and it was put up here in eighteen seventy two.
Look at how much of it there is. It's a huge thing, put together from parts like a kit.
Water was switched off in two thousand and eight. The fountain was closed in July two thousand and seventeen for a restoration costing one point nine million pounds, and it was restored in twenty eighteen. If the water's running when you look at it, that's the result.
